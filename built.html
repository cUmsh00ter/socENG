What is Social Engineering?

A complete learning experience designed for clarity, progress, and lasting results.

Phishing

The most common form. Attackers send fraudulent messages that appear to come from trusted sources (banks, companies, government agencies) to trick victims into clicking malicious links, opening infected attachments, or entering credentials on fake sites.

Pretexting

The attacker invents a plausible scenario(pretext) and impersonates a trusted figure (e.g., IT Support, bank employee, colleague, or authority) to extract information or gain access. It often involves prior research to make the story convincing.

Baiting

Attackers lure victims with something desirable (free software, music, movies, or a found USB drive labeled something intriguing). The "bait" delivers malware or leads to credential theft when the victim takes the offer.

Tailgating/ Piggybacking

A physical attack in which the attacker follows an authorized person into a restricted area (office building, data center, etc.) without proper authentication, often by acting as if they belong or asking someone to hold the door.

Scareware

Fake alerts or pop-ups that frighten the user into believing their device is infected or at risk, prompting them to download malicious "antivirus" software or pay for a fraudulent fix.

Business Email Compromise/ Invoice Fraud

Attackers impersonate executives, vendors, or business partners (often after compromising or spoofing an email account) to trick employees into transfering money or sharing sensitive data. Related techniques include brand impersonation.
